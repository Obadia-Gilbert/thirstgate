Where our water facts come from

Thirstgate shares general health information, not medical advice. If you have a condition that affects how much you should drink, follow your doctor’s advice.

Sharper focus

Even mild dehydration can make it harder to concentrate and leave you in a worse mood.

  • Ganio MS, et al. Mild dehydration impairs cognitive performance and mood of men. British Journal of Nutrition. 2011;106(10):1535–1543.
  • Armstrong LE, et al. Mild dehydration affects mood in healthy young women. Journal of Nutrition. 2012;142(2):382–388.

Fewer headaches

Not drinking enough is a common headache trigger.

  • Blau JN. Water deprivation: a new migraine precipitant. Headache. 2005;45(6):757–759.
  • NHS. Headaches.

Steadier energy

Tiredness and low energy can be signs of not drinking enough.

  • Armstrong LE, et al. Mild dehydration affects mood in healthy young women. Journal of Nutrition. 2012;142(2):382–388.
  • NHS. Dehydration.

Healthier kidneys

Water helps your kidneys clear waste, and drinking enough lowers the risk of kidney stones.

  • National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases. Eating, Diet, & Nutrition for Kidney Stones.
  • Borghi L, et al. Urinary volume, water and recurrences in idiopathic calcium nephrolithiasis: a 5-year randomized prospective study. Journal of Urology. 1996;155(3):839–843.

Safe limits

Thirstgate stops counting after 1 litre in an hour or 3.5 litres in a day, because drinking very large amounts quickly can be harmful.

  • NHS. Water, drinks and your health.
